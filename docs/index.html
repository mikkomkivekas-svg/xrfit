Incident spectrum

Simplified model: Kramers continuum plus the anode K lines (none if the voltage is below the anode's K edge, as for W under 69.5 kV), with no window or filter absorption. For an exact spectrum, compute it elsewhere and load it from a file.

Beam filters between the source and the sample

The weight is the relative photon intensity at that energy and sets the line intensities of each element. Add scatter to a line to fit a Rayleigh and a Compton peak for it. Delete a line to remove that energy.

Sample matrix

Calibrates the tube on a standard: give every amount (no ?), and K and the share of the anode K lines in the incident spectrum are fitted. Needs a spectrum generated from the X-ray tube.

Filters before the detector

Layer 1 faces the beam and detector. The last layer is infinitely thick. Write each element as Fe 70 (wt %), Cr ? (found from the fitted lines) or O balance (the rest). A formula such as SiO2 works too. The same element may be unknown in several layers if you tick Fit α, β and γ lines separately (Kα and Kβ are absorbed differently, which tells the depths apart). Needs a fit first.

Elements

An element picks K or L lines from what the incident spectrum reaches. Write Pb-L for a shell or Pb-La2 for one line.
